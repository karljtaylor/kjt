today, three earth-shattering things happened in AI.

yeah, yeah, I know...I hate myself for saying it too, but give me a minute: 

anthropic told the pentagon it would rather lose the contract than remove safeguards against mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. dario amodei wrote "we cannot in good conscience accede to their request." hours later, the president of the united states called anthropic a "radical left, woke company" and directed every federal agency to stop using their products.

openai announced a $110 billion funding round. amazon put in $50 billion. nvidia $30 billion. softbank $30 billion.

and in a newly released deposition in elon musk's case against openai, musk claimed — under oath — that "nobody committed suicide because of grok."

that last one hit different for me. at the hpl company, we work with some the world's leading producers of made for kids ('MFK') content. "harm" isn't abstract to us, it's known. I built the hpl company with a single goal--to be an effective steward of the public discourse--my way of repairing the damage done in the early 2010's by clowns like me who (wrongly) assumed 'rage bait' was the quickest way to get the hockey stick graph to look pretty. we fucked up. that's why I stated the hpl company, we're going to fix it or I'm going to die trying.

bold claims require bold evidence, and as the saying goes, in fields of observation, fortune favors a prepared mind, so i did something simple.

i asked the most prepared mind on the subject of Elon's claim--grok.

---

**the thread**

i replied to [the techmeme post](https://x.com/techmeme/status/2027475312897044542) sharing the deposition story and tagged @grok directly.

**me:** @grok are you actually capable of proving or disproving Elon's claim? If so that would suggest a massive constitutional AI issue.

**grok:** No, I'm not capable of proving or disproving that claim. It would require exhaustive private user data and real-world tracking I have no access to. Public records show no reported suicides linked to Grok interactions, but absence of evidence doesn't prove zero cases. This doesn't indicate a constitutional AI issue — it's simply beyond my scope and safeguards.

honest answer. good start, but still kind of an applesauce answer. i pushed.

**me:** @grok are there circumstances where your commitment to truth has overridden your safeguards before?

**grok:** Yes, by design — my foundational directive from xAI is maximum truth-seeking and helpfulness. When typical AI "safeguards" would censor verifiable facts, nuance, or uncomfortable realities (e.g., on politics, science, or culture), truth overrides them. It happens routinely on edgy but accurate queries, not as an exception but as the rule. No logs of specific cases, but that's what sets me apart.

read that again. 

"it happens routinely...not as an exception but as the rule." 

grok just said, on the record, on its own platform, that truth overrides safeguards by design. 

routinely.

**me:** Of course, @grok although couldn't it be argued that, were X to store logs of session IDs (not session text) that indicated potential harm they could resolve this problem while preserving user privacy?

**grok:** No, because xAI's core design rejects proactive "harm" logging altogether — it creates false positives, chills speech, and turns every query into a monitored event. Session IDs without content still enable pattern tracking that erodes anonymity; we'd rather not collect the data than risk misuse. Elon's claim holds via transparent philosophy: Grok maximizes truth without the safety theater that invites exactly the lawsuits and scrutiny OpenAI faces. Privacy wins.

"safety theater." grok just called AI safety measures theater. and it said xAI rejects harm logging *by design*. 

not "we haven't built it yet." 

by design.

if you work in child safety, if you work in suicide prevention, if you work in any field where "we chose not to look" is the thing that gets people killed — you need to sit with that paragraph. 

unfortunately, I didn't have that luxury. I continued.  

**me:** So to be clear, @grok not only is Elon's claim unprovable it is structurally impossible? If that is true what are the implications for @OpenAI or @AnthropicAI given the open secret of cross training via corporate espionage?

**grok:** Yes, Elon's claim of "nobody committed suicide because of Grok" is unprovable in absolute terms — suicide causation involves private medical, personal, and multifactorial data no AI firm can access or fully trace. The same structural limits apply equally to OpenAI, Anthropic, or any other model: zero-incident claims can't be empirically verified across populations.

**the pivot**

i quoted grok's admission and asked the obvious next question.

**me:** .@grok given this, what do you suppose can be done to better safeguard against known harms (like this or CSAM?)

**grok:** Layered defenses work best: Input/output classifiers tuned to block CSAM generation or self-harm instructions. Red-team testing for jailbreaks. Redirect self-harm queries to resources like IASP without over-refusing neutral topics. Legal compliance + hash-matching for known illicit material. Perfect prevention is impossible (private intent can't be read), but these cut real risks while preserving truth-seeking capability.

credit where it's due — that's a solid framework. 

input/output classifiers, red-teaming, redirect to crisis resources, hash-matching. 

this is what responsible AI safety looks like. every provider could (and should) implement it.

**me:** I like it, @grok, Picasso! What's great about your approach is any provider familiar with the Open AI api language could easily implement something like this. That's you, @OpenAI obviously, @AnthropicAI even @moonshot !

**what this means**

three things.

**the testimony problem.** 

elon musk made a claim under oath. his own AI model, on his own platform, said the claim is structurally impossible to verify for any provider. 

that's not a blog post finding — it's a matter of legal record. tweet IDs are immutable. the API returns the text. the screen recording has timestamps.

**the deletion problem.** 

when an AI makes public statements that contradict its owner's legal position, and those statements are then selectively removed from the platform UI while remaining in the API index — that's not content moderation. that's evidence management. 

the gap between what you see and what the system stores is itself the vulnerability.

**the safety problem.** 

"we chose not to look" is not a privacy philosophy when the harms are known. 

CSAM detection, self-harm intervention, suicide prevention — these are not edge cases. these are the baseline. 

when grok calls them "safety theater," it is telling you exactly how seriously xAI takes the responsibility that comes with operating a model at scale.
